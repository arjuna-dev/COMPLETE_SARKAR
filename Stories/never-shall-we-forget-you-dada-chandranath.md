### NOTE FROM THE PUBLISHER

The biggest reward of human life is the company of the good and the great. All other benefits have a diminishing return, but the fruits of satsaôga grow forever.

I am fortunate enough to have such a very close, family, and personal relationship with Ácárya Chandranathji. Bábá really encouraged the linking in marriage of my eldest son, Anand Bihari, with Geeta, Ácárya Chandranathji's daughter. For my family of traditional values and caste-bound mindset, it was really revolutionary to agree to a marriage outside our caste. To begin with, we were outcaste and socially isolated, but soon the tide turned in our favour and thereafter almost all marriages in the family were arranged outside the caste and without dowry.

During the Emergency, I was quite downcast. My service was all but terminated and I had to flee from place to place to avoid arrest. I made one of the escape trips to Hazaribagh in Bihar, where Ácáryaji was the Principal of the prestigious training college for the Bihar Military Police.

Bihar state was quite hostile to Ánanda Márga and I was surprised that a doyen of Ánanda Márga like Chandranathji was out of trouble there. Upon enquiry I was informed that the government had decided to arrest Ácáryaji and even decided upon the prison in which he would be lodged. But then the Inspector General Police had warned the government that Chandranathji was so popular amongst the entire constabulary in the state that he feared an armed rebellion, as had already happened in the state of Uttar Pradesh some time before, if Chandranathji was arrested. Wise counsel thus prevailed and he was not touched.

After retirement I tried to persuade Ácáryaji to give me the facts of any harassment so that I could pursue his rightful claims to compensation with the Chief Secretary of the state. But Ácáryaji showed no interest in this matter, even though the Governor of Bihar, Shri VC Pandey was a close friend and colleague. But Ácáryaji

would not attend to any of these mundane matters.

After my retirement from service, I wanted to run a small manufacturing unit in Jaipur, but when I met Bábá in Calcutta, he advised me to follow the example of Chandranathji and devote my time only to spiritual and intellectual pursuits. I followed suit and sold off my unit immediately.

Very recently, when I mentioned to Dadaji about the Machiavellian ways of some of the senior dadas of the Márga, and expressed my apprehension that this might harm the Márga, he was visibly disturbed and said in a loud and clear voice, "No power on Earth can harm Ánanda Márga. The conspirators would bite the dust as did the perpetrators of the Emergency".

I remember these words of his whenever my mind gets depressed and the current goings-on in the Márga.

I feel grateful that I have been given the task of translating this historic book.

May Dada live a full hundred years to guide, comfort and protect us.

Mangal Bihari
21ˢᵗ October 2006

# GRATITUDE

When devotion in myriad streams gushes forth from the heart of a devotee night and day, in every direction, the sweet vibrations from the is't'a begin to overpower him or her and he/she becomes oblivious of his/her own existence. Only the Beloved remains. Immersed in the is't'a, only the Beloved remains within and without.

Chandranathdada is an example and witness to this kind of rare existence. In every action of his, one experiences Bábá's presence, that infinite personality at Whose sweet lotus feet he has surrendered everything.

Right from his darshan of Bábá and initiation, he has been experiencing Sannidhya (closeness), sálokya (constant presence), sárúpya (oneness), sáyujya (merging) and kaevalya (singleness) with Him. It appears that every moment of his life is full of Bábá, full of the graceful play of his Beloved Master.

For the last ten to twelve years I have had the good luck of speaking and listening to Chandranathdada from time to time. You can say that it has been my good fortune that I have known a little about this great devotee.

On one occasion, Chandranathdada told me, "Once Rev. Shraddhánanda Dada, who is now Purodhá Pramukha, gave me a big notebook. He insisted that I write my experiences with Bábá in that notebook. This will help the devotees of the whole world to know about Bábá's early programmes and work and be a source of inspiration for them".

Dada Chandranathji accepted Shraddhánanda Dada's request. It was the time before Bábá gave up His physical body. In the times Shraddhánanda Dada was asking about, Chandranathji was staying most of the time with Bábá, spending his entire time following Bábá's directions and instructions. He translated Bábá's Bengali works into Hindi starting in 1955 with *Ánanda Márga*, and continued later with translations of *Namah Shiváya Shántáya* and

*Prabhat Saḿgiita*. The series of Tattva Sabhas or philosophical meetings and initiations, became longer and longer.

This was dada's spiritual life. Once referring to his preoccupations with these duties, he told Bábá, "Bábá, so many people come to talk about spirituality and initiation that sometimes I have some difficulty in giving sufficient time for my sádhaná."

At this, Bábá gave a charming smile and putting a caressing hand on Dada's cheek, He said, "Chandranath, works of social welfare are also part and parcel of sádhaná. This will help you in progressing on the path of sádhaná".

After these instructions from Bábá, Dada gave up his hesitations and dedicated himself to Bábá's work with redoubled vigour. I have seen him working hard up to eight hours continuously, even in this old age. There would be a long line of visitors from India and abroad to meet with him and he would meet the ordinary sádhaka or the practitioners of visheïa yoga all with the same affection and sincerity. Once I had a conversation with a practitioner of visheïa yoga who had been coming to him for years. He said, "Every time I return from Dada's I am full of satisfaction, but when I come back I realise that I have to receive much, much more still. In the same way as when we go into a deep mine, the deeper we go the more riches (experiences) rain on us".

There is an incident that occurred in 1955-56. A resident of Trimuhan, which is close to Bhagalpur, Shri Aniruddha Dadaji, was a brother disciple of Ácáryaji and he had got everyone in his family, including his father, brother and wife, initiated by Ácáryaji. Anniruddhaji wanted to take Ácáryaji to conduct a Tattva Sabha, but for some reason Ácáryaji could not go there. Anniruddhaji felt angry about this and went to Bábá to express his dissatisfaction. What he experienced there surprised him, however, for when he went to touch Bábá's feet, Bábá turned the other way.

At last Anniruddhaji asked Bábá in Angika, "Bábá, why are you doing like this today?"

Bábá replied, "You are very angry with Chandranath, and how can I bear that?" Then Bábá added, "Do you know Chandranath?" And after a pause He added, "He is the Pacific Ocean!"

After contacting Aniruddha Dada, I also came to know that there was a Dharma Mahá Cakra in his village of Trimuhan in 1958 and there were some unusual features of this DMC. Firstly, it was arranged during the day, and afterwards Bábá left the village.

Secondly, *Renaissance Universal* started from the time of this DMC and the evening before the DMC, Bábá gave the first Presidential address of the Renaissance Universal platform. Bábá had planned the Renaissance Artists' and Writers' Association (RAWA) programme the previous evening also. There was an enactment of a drama by Rabindranath Tagore done under Bábá's own direction and conducted by Ácárya Dr Sachinandan Mandal. During this performance there was a song:

> *"If no-one hears your call and comes to you, start alone!*
> *Start alone! Move ahead alone!"*

Move ahead, do not stop. Move forward. (Here I must add that although Bábá had prohibited ácáryas from acting, but with Bábá's special permission, Ácárya Mandal acted in that drama).

Later Bábá told a story to Anniruddhaji why this drama was so hastily organised within a couple of days of this function.

"Aniruddha, would you not like to know why during DMC at your place, this two day's function was organised, and why straight after the DMC in the day, I took leave of you?

Close your eyes! See back about three thousand five hundred years ago. What do you see on your mental plate?"

Dada looked and saw Bábá in the form of Shrii Kŕshna had come at the invitation of the then king, Karna. Aniruddhaji saw that he had been a commander in King Karna's army and was engaged in extending hospitality to Lord Kŕshna. He was requesting Krishna to stay over for the night and give more opportunities to them to serve Him.

Shrii Kŕshna accepted this request and spent the night there. For His entertainment, a poetic dance-drama was enacted that night. The next day, after His midday meal, Kŕshna bid farewell, and while going, Anniruddhaji had requested Kŕshna to come again. Dada's hospitality had pleased Kŕshna.

Then Bábá said, "Look Anniruddha! I had come to you to fulfil that promise. This is the very same place where in your past life you had served me. In memory of that I have started RAWA at your place and finished the DMC during the day, and bade farewell to you".

Ácárya Chandranathji says Táraka Brahma never leaves a promise unfulfilled.

Dada was so much dedicated to Bábá, that He had to come to Dada again and again, sometimes on the pretext of DMC, sometimes for a surprise inspection, on any day and at any time. In the days when there were only a few Márgiis, DMC used to be held at Dada's residence in Bhagalpur, but when the numbers increased, then Bábá would stay at Dada's residence in Nathnagar and DMC would be held at some other convenient place.

Dada says that at that time, to elucidate His talks, Bábá used to demonstrate the talks in a wonderful, otherworldly manner. Once Bábá was delivering a discourse at Dada's residence on '*Action and Its Reactions*'. In this context, Bábá called Dada and told him, "Action, whether good or bad, has results which have to be compulsorily borne by the doer. Without bearing the reactions, the action does not disappear for hundreds and thousands of ages even". Then Bábá added, "Nature follows its rules under the subordination of the Almighty, and people have to undergo the reactions of their actions. But if the Almighty wants, He can deliver people from their reactions and free them. In this regard, Dada has always repeated these words of Prabhat Saîgiita: -

> *T'ar man Jadi c'a'y Sab kichu hay*
> *Dhu'limay e dharaniite,*
> *Moha'na' ha'te nadii Paha're Phere Jay*
> *Sa Jadi ca'y Pa'sa'n ka'ra'y*
> *Phul phota'le Pa're go.*

If He desires, anything can happen on this dry and dusty earth.

A river can return to the mountain, having reached the end of its flow.

If He thinks, He can make the flower bloom in the stony prison house.

In that same DMC Bábá gave new life to Bindeshwari Dada and thus, as Parama Puruśa He changed the normal laws of action and reaction.

Bábá used to say, "How much can a human being do? Therefore you surrender yourself unto me. Herein lies the utility of your life".

Dada has absorbed and digested this fundamental formula absolutely. Hence in spite of innumerable difficulties, he continued to proceed on Bábá's path without caring for respect or criticism. Kicking away fear and shyness, tolerating family anxieties and difficulties, he went ahead according to the direction instructed by His is't'a. On Bábá's orders, he performed inter-caste marriages and as a result the people of his village did everything to oppose him, socially boycotted him, and prohibited people from working in his farm and lands. These and many similar troubles continued to come his way, but Dada continued to fight all like a soldier of Bábá, calmly and quietly. By Bábá's grace, whether it was the government or society, everyone had to succumb and Dada never allowed the rose of Bábá's face to become dusty or dark. What else can the one whom Parama Purusa favours desire from any one?

There is another incident when Bábá suddenly arrived for a surprise inspection at Dada's house. Bábá lay down on Dada's bed and said, "Chandranath, note down this poem of Rabindranath Tagor,

> "*Ei Kareche, Bha`lo Nithur, Ei Kareche Bha`lo!*
> *Emani Kare Ridhay A'me'r, Tii'bra da'han Ja'lo!*"

> *Ama'r Edhu'p Na Po'rale, gandha Kichuii Na da'le*
> *Ama'r E diip Na Ja'la'le, daii Na kichuii Tumi.*
> *Jekhan Takhe Achetane E chitta Ama'r*
> *A'gha't seje Paras Tabo', Sii To' Puraskar.*
> *Andha'kare Mohela'ge cakhya toma'e dekhinago*
> *Bajra Tule A'gun Jale, Amar Sethaii kale."*

Dada summed up the substance of the song like this, "O Parama Purus'a, whenever I am hurt by circumstances, it appears that through them I am also receiving Your vibrations. O my Beloved Lord, You alone know how You come into my life in so many ways to bless me! I have got You and nothing else remains to be achieved".

Whenever I look into Dada's eyes I am lost in their depths. I am face to face with a very pious and simple heart. Whatever the problem, Dada sees every solution in Bábá. Whenever we ask for something, he opens that very page of Bábá's book, as if Bábá Himself is giving the solution.

Dada often says, "Bábá has left none of His responsibilities on others. Whether it was His family or social responsibilities or spiritual responsibilities, Bábá fulfilled all of them in His lifetime. He came on this Earth for a mission that He has fulfilled. Only wait for the right opportune time. The curtain will be lifted and people will see that the whole universe has become full of Bábá.

A beautiful dawn with hope and trust has arrived. The darkness of exploitation is disappearing. This rudderless country and society had been filled up with the life-breath of the Great One. Let us do sastaunga pranám to Him repeating following lines in the mind:

> *"Ashru Muche A'na'be ha'nsi*
> *Ka'na' sure Ba'jabo go ba'nsii*
> *Matiir Pare A'sabesudin,*
> *Klesh Ja'tana' karo'robena'."*

Now the weeping days and tears will become the things of past.
With smiles coming all the way.
Sweet melodies of His flute will vibrate the heart.
The Golden dawn will herald this dusty earth
With sufferings and tortures never standing on the Path.

**Humbly**
**Dadan**

# CHAPTER ONE

### *God is Infinite and Infinite are the Tales about Him*

So far I have seen eighty-eight springs in my life. The year 1953 was my thirty-fifth year. That spring was the crimson dawn of my life. I was in search of a guru and it was in that year that I could find Him. When one yearns strongly for salvation, one gets the Sadguru.

Prabhát Ranjan Sarkar, who came to be called Bábá, was living in a rented house in the Keshavpur locality of Jamalpur. It was in this house that I was eventually initiated.

After initiation, Bábá told me, “Follow Yama and Niyama strictly. Let your face always be illuminated with spiritual light”.

After that, through a brief conversation, He gave me subtle spiritual instructions in the following manner.

He Himself asked the questions, “Where did the Swan (Soul) come from? Where does it go? And where does it remain immersed? Where is it lost?”

He answered, “The Swan comes from the unqualified Nirguńa Brahma, gets involved in the qualified Saguńa Brahma, and it gets attached to the body where it gets lost in máyá.”

When I was ready to leave, Bábá said, “Do the maximum sádhana that you can and serve the creatures of this world. Search every opportunity to serve the people.”

Then He said, “Tulsi said, ‘When you came into the world you were crying and the world laughed. Perform such actions that when you leave, the world weeps and you laugh.’”

Days passed by and gradually I learnt all the lessons of Sahaj Yoga. I was doing my sádhaná regularly and now and then, by His Grace, I was also getting spiritual realisations. People who came in contact with me came to know about my sádhaná practices. Those who used to come to me, were impressed by my talk and conduct, and expressed their desire to take initiation. But in those days, the rule was that without Bábá’s permission, we could neither take anyone to Him, nor inform anyone of His address. I used to go to Jamalpur to seek His permission, and then sent to Him those whose names He approved, and would ask the others to wait.

Time passed and in 1954, by His Grace, my wife became restive to take initiation. Seeing her strong desire, I went to Bábá to seek His permission, which He gave, and asked that she be brought soon and she was then initiated.

In the month of November that year, Bábá started giving weekly discourses. Everyone He had initiated would gather in a house in Rampur colony and there Bábá would give His discourse. We all used to gather there to meet Him. The Railways had allotted this house to Bábá’s brother, Kanaida, but Bábá was living in a rented house and Kanaida had given this house to us to be used as a jágrti or ashram, as we had none of our own.

Ánanda Márga was established in 1955. That year on the 1st January, the first day of the international calendar, a festivity was observed. Bábá gave a discourse, which was published in *Subháśita Saḿgraha*, and then there was a collective feast. We took food for Bábá to His residence and we had our meal together in that Railway Quarter. The discourse that day was ‘*The Gradual Progress of Society*’. This is published as the first speech in *Subháśita Saḿgraha Part One*.

In the month of February, Dharma Mahá Cakra was held for the first time outside Jamalpur, in the Sergeant Major’s quarter in the Police Lines at Bhagalpur. As soon as he came to the quarter, Bábá initiated all the children of my family, my old aunt and our domestic help, Makhanji. After dinner Bábá left for Jamalpur.

After Bábá had left, all the brothers and sisters who had come to attend the DMC sat down together to have their meal. Everyone complained that there was no salt in the food, but Bábá had eaten the food without salt. My wife and our domestic help Makhanji had jointly cooked the food and both of them are great devotees of Bábá. The following week I went to Jamalpur and asked Bábá’s pardon for serving Him food without salt, but Bábá said that He had not noticed that the food had no salt. He said that the food was tasty and that He had eaten His fill. I was surprised to hear Bábá say this. He is so kind to His devotees.

Again in 1955, on 6th May on the day of the full moon of the month of Vaeshákha, the first Ánanda Púrńimá DMC was celebrated in Monghyr, and Bábá gave His discourse at noon. After that, lunch was served to Bábá and all the brothers and sisters had a meal together sitting in a group. By evening everyone left for home.

By now, the book, *Ánanda Márga*, had been published in Bengali as well as its Hindi translation. This is the story of this first primary book of philosophy of Ánanda Márga.

Bábá dictated the Bengali text to Shivashankarji. After the book was published in Bengali from Bhagalpur, Shivashankarji gave a copy to me, with the instruction from Bábá, that this was to be translated into Hindi. I was surprised. To do a translation from Bengali to Hindi seemed like a very difficult task! However, I started the work with courage. As the work of translation began, it seemed unexpectedly easy, and as it proceeded, it became easier still and was finished quickly. According to instructions, it was published in Bhagalpur and I hurriedly sent a few copies to Jamalpur with my wife.

1955 is an important year in the history of Ánanda Márga. The first Dharma Mahá Cakra of Ánanda Púrńimá had been observed in the month of May and before, that some of us had become ácáryas. Bábá started training ácáryas in February 1955.

Bábá told me, “Chandranath, I shall make you people ácáryas. You people will initiate and give initiation to a large number of people. This will not be possible with just my physical body.”

In July of this same year Bábá taught me Vishesha Yoga. As I have been experiencing all the time, Bábá has always been kind to me.

In April 1955 I was transferred from Bhagalpur to Nathnagar. Nathnagar is a sub-town of Bhagalpur, where there is a Police Training Centre. In this post, I began to have enough free time for sádhaná, spiritual talk and giving initiation. Along with this I also used to get an opportunity for Bábá’s weekly darshan in Jamalpur every Sunday. So many people were initiated, that in 1958 Bábá gave me the honour of being called Dharma Mitram. Later I would select capable persons from amongst those I initiated and Bábá would train them and make them ácáryas. After being transferred to other places, these ácáryas spread Ánanda Márga far and wide. In Bihar, units of Ánanda Márga Pracáraka Sangha were established almost everywhere. *Ánanda Márga Pracáraka Saḿgha* is the name of the society while *Ánanda Márga* is a philosophy. Those days so many people became ácáryas from Bhagalpur that Reverend Bábá named Bhagalpur the centre for making ácáryas. Some of the ácáryas of those days were: -

Sarva Shrii Akhori Himanchal Prasad, Ram Vilas Rai, Dharmendra Rao, Devichand Sharma, Pashupati Datta, Shyam Sundarlal Das, Tarkeshwar Prasad, Jwala Prasad Singh, Ananda Thakur, Krishnanand Jha etc. etc.

These ácáryas propagated and spread Ánanda Márga in different district headquarters of Bihar.

That year Bábá instructed me to organize a Tattva Sabha (philosophical meeting), in a far-flung village of Bihar, called Gaddopur. Some ácáryas and lady ácáryás were instructed to help me. These were Sarva Shrii Shivashankar Bannerjee, Hari Sadhan Ghosh, Ram Tanuk Singh and lady ácáryás, Rampari Devii and Ahilya Devii. I reached Gaddopur along with these people.

Before we left for Gaddopur, Bábá called me and asked me to write down some questions and their answers. This meeting of spiritual seekers had a large number of traditional pandits and purohits. Bábá’s instructions were that the male ácáryas would give the speeches while the lady ácáryás would go from door to door to talk with the ladies in their houses, and I was to answer questions. After the speeches, the pandits started asking questions. One by one I gave satisfactory answers to all the questions. The most wonderful thing was that the questions were the very same ones that Bábá had already dictated to me, to which he had also

given the answers. I had prepared to achieve 100% marks in this examination. Let us see the miracle of that Great Magician and Táraka Brahma Bábá! The meeting thus concluded successfully. By evening there was a long line of aspirants for taking initiation at my door. The work of initiation continued all night up to the morning. All the four male ácáryas were completely occupied. The lady ácáryás going from house to house also initiated many ladies. Parama Puruśa remained over kind to this very small village.

A jágrti was constructed here by 1961. Bábá promised to visit this village. Parama Puruśa never leaves anything half done only we are unable to cope with His speed and lag behind. Bábá gave His special grace to this small place. In October 1979 there was a Dharma Mahá Cakra in this village. I had requested Bábá to come to Gaddopur, soon after He was released from jail and He readily agreed to my request.

How can I describe His grace? He is infinite and the stories of Him are also infinite. Soon after I was posted at Nathnagar, Bábá began a series of surprise visits to my place. This continued all the time I was posted there. For the entire five years of my service career that I remained in Nathnagar, Bábá would appear before me on any day, at any time, to put me to the test. What I attained by His long proximity and these many tests is beyond description. All that I can say is that I attained the unattainable, the impossible became possible and I received His very special grace. Let me tell just one incident from those days.

A jágrti had been constructed in Bhagalpur, which was the first jágrti of Ánanda Márga. One Sunday evening I had gone to the jágrti, which was at a distance of about four miles from my house, to participate in the weekly Dharma Cakra. My eldest son Amarnath and our domestic help Makhanji were the only ones left in the house when Amarnathji suddenly fell grievously ill. On receiving the news, the principal of the Police Training School came with his wife and they called the doctor who began treatment.

Amarnathji was induced to sleep with the help of drugs. His mother Ácáryá Rampari Devi had gone to Gaddopur and when I returned late in the night from the Dharma Cakra, Makhanji told me everything. Bábá replied, “Do not forget that you are only my medium. It is my responsibility³⁶ to see the spiritual progress of Ánanda Márgiis. The selected mantras are also siddha mantras³⁷. And if sádhakas practice with them regularly, the results will certainly come. You must have seen the sentient³⁸ look of many Ánanda Márgiis”.

Rtambhar asked, “Please give me Savikalpa and Nirvikalpa Samádhi!³⁹”

Bábá replied, “You have experienced these; some months ago I demonstrated samádhi making you my medium⁴⁰”.

Rtambhar replied, “Yes I remember. In 1954 you conducted the demonstration of death⁴¹ and restoration of life before me. At that time you made the historical announcement that you are Shrii Shrii Ánandamúrti”. (In this experiment Shri Krishna Chandra Pal was the medium).

Bábá said, “Now try for savikalpa and nirvikalpa samádhi by yourself. Be your own lamp!”

Ánanda Márga Pracáraka Saḿgha was established in 1954. Shri Prabhát Rainjan Sarkar was elected as President unanimously. He selected Rtambhar as General Secretary. He was also selected as the P.A. of Ánandamúrtijii⁴². After some months Rtambhar became, Jiivamitram⁴³ and Dharmamitram⁴⁴ and Purodhá⁴⁵.

As time passed Rtambhar became busy as General Secretary. He could not spare much time to give initiations or for sádhaná. Here it is expedient to state that giving initiation to suitable persons is service of the vipra⁴⁶ or intellectual category. In a larger context it is also Brahma-sádhaná. But the work of General Secretary of Ánanda Márga Pracáraka Saḿgha does not come in any of these categories.

This can be called shúdrocita sevá or physical service, which is a part of Karma Yoga⁴⁷. Rtambhar became fully involved in the establishment of Ánanda Márga Pracáraka Saḿgha. He began to propagate the ideals of Ánanda Márga and the greatness and the generosity of Ánandamúrtijii. He was following these two principles of Karma Yoga: 1) Renouncing the doership of action and 2) Surrendering every action to Brahma. But he was not giving importance to the third principle to surrender the fruits of the action to the Supreme although in every action he used to feel “Lord I am your slave—I am your slave”⁴⁸.

One day Márga Guru instructed Rtambhar to initiate two young men as avadhútas⁴⁹. These two persons were Sudhiir Kumar and Shivaji. This was perhaps the month of May 1962. They were given ochre clothes⁵⁰ and their names were changed to Abhedánanda Avadhúta and Dhyanánanda Avadhúta.

In 1962 in Calcutta, Rtambhar married the daughter of a former film director Shri Ranjeet Bannerjee, named Pratima Devi. Márga Guru himself was present in the marriage. Although Bábá had stopped initiating people Himself, Bábá initiated Rtambhar’s wife Himself two or three days after the marriage. She is so fortunate in this matter.

In March 1964, Márga Guru instructed Rtambhar to go to Baglata in Purulia district. There, as per plan to render needed service to the public.

The Raja of Garh Jaipur, Raghunandan Singh Deva, donated a big piece of land to Ánanda Márga. This land was in a wild area where Ánanda Nagar⁵¹ was to be established.

Ácárya Arun Kumar (now Svarupánanda Avadhúta)⁵² had initiated Rtambhar’s mother. In February 1965 she passed away while doing her sádhaná. Rtambhar was brought in a special car from Ánanda Nagar to Jamalpur to perform her funeral rites. The death of his mother shook Rtambhar deeply, but he appeared calm and normal before avadhútas and Márgii brothers and sisters. He commemorated the departure of his mother and recalled the following shloka to help him bear the shock and give him forbearance:

> *Antkále ca máma eva smaranmuktvá kalevaram*
> *Prayáti sa madhshávam yáti nástyam sańshayah*

> ‘There is no doubt that one who, at the time of death, leaves the body remembering Me attains Me.’⁵³

After performing the shraddhá⁵⁴ ceremony as per Ánanda Márga rites, Rtambhar returned to Ánanda Nagar. Here he dedicated himself with full sincerity to the building of the township.

The mind of a sádhaka becomes prey to avidyá máyá⁵⁵ and Rtambhar was not an exception to this. He formed his own opinion on renunciation and becoming an avadhúta and also on the project township of Ánanda Nagar. For this reason he began to differ with Márga Guru on these matters. Márga Guru regarded this as indiscipline.

Rtambhar’s conduct was becoming more and more egotistical and Márga Guru did not tolerate this. He relieved Rtambhar of his post, and he returned to Jamalpur.

In April 1976 Rtambhar sold his ancestral house⁵⁶ in Bhagalpur and shifted to Balia in Howrah district permanently. Gurudev shifted to Madhumalaiócha in the suburb of Lake Gardens⁵⁷ in Calcutta, however He had not forgotten Rtambhar.

Rtambhar suffered a paralytic stroke in April 1975. But by the grace of Gurudev he regained his health. In some people’s opinion, Gurudev extended Rtambhar’s life. In the middle of 1989, Gurudev remembered Rtambhar and He sent a special Bhagalpur mango to him as prasád. This was Bábá’s kindness. Fifty years ago, out of His love for him, Bábá used to sometimes give Rtambhar sweets. When Bábá’s messengers came with the Bhagalpur mango, Rtambhar was lost in old memories. He began to remember those events of the past. Recalling Bábá’s love for him during the Jamalpur days intoxicated him.

Seeing Rtambhar lost in himself his wife told the messenger, “My husband has lost his normal consciousness. I receive Bábá’s gift with devotion and thankfulness”.

After some days Bábá sent some apple from Shimla and also conveyed the message that Rtambhar should go and see the development of Ánanda Nagar himself. But Rtambhar could not take advantage of this opportunity.

On 22nd October 1990 Rtambhar read in the newspapers the news of the Maháprayáńa⁵⁸ of Gurudev. This was the end of a great man’s active life. Rtambhar was very sad. This was like a bolt from the blue for him. He told himself:

> ‘*Thou hast need of thy nearest creature,*
> *Thou hast need of what once was mine,*
> *The thirst that consumes my spirit,*
> *Is the thirst of Thy heart for mine*’

# CHAPTER THREE

I am turning over the pages of the past, trying to put down the events appearing before my eyes before all is lost and forgotten. It is possible that I may have already forgotten many things which will be lost forever in the recesses of the mind and may never come to light. I have committed a great mistake by not putting my experiences in writing at the time. I am now realizing this. In the earlier days, I never dreamed that any institution will come into being and that it will be so big; that His ideals will spread all over the world and wipe the tears of suffering humanity in every village and home. I was simply satisfied that, I am, there is my sádhaná, and that my Guru is "The Creator, the Sustainer and the Great Destroyer¹" of all. But today these lines are ringing in my ears:

> 'After the tears there will be smiles
> All ears will hear Lord’s flute
> The good days will return for this earth
> Do not grieve or feel pain in your heart'²

Gurudev³, (those days I was calling Bábá, Gurudev), said that the aim of human life is "Self-realization and service to humanity". Human existence is only meaningful by following this ideal. Now I am realizing this.

By leading us step by step, the Universal Teacher, Shrii Shrii Ánandamúrtijii has guided us to this point. When I look back from this promontory, my mind is filled with wonder and these lines of the poet Keshava⁴ come forcefully to my mind:

> 'Oh Lord! It is not possible to relate or describe everything.
> After looking at your wonderful creation one has to realize it and be silent'.

It was the spring of 1953 – the month of March. This spring transformed the whole of my life into spring. I was in search of a guru. I came into contact with the Great Advent. It is correct that:

"When the desire for salvation is strong, the sadguru appears" ⁵.

In those days I was living in Dumka, headquarters of the Santhal Subdivision⁶ of Bihar⁷. I was a sergeant major⁸ there in charge of the Police Department’s Reserve Office. I received the information that an old Sub-inspector had been appointed to that office. I approached the Police Superintendent and opposed this appointment. I felt that this old sub-inspector would either like to stay in a police station or be in a

position from where he could get promotion and that he would not work wholeheartedly in a dry place like the headquarters of a Reserve Office. The Police Superintendent explained that this officer wanted to work voluntarily in the Reserve Office and felt he should be given a chance. I was surprised to hear this and agreed. I wanted to see the man, whose name was Shivashankar Bandopadhyay, and talk to him.

Ácárya Bannerjee⁹ Dada - as he was famously known in those days, is no longer in this world, but he initiated a lot of people during his life. He came then and took over the charge of the office. His manner of work was sincere and his gentlemanliness, honesty, conduct, and behaviour impressed me. I was convinced that this man must certainly be in contact with some great, divine personality. I used to call him to my residence after office hours and talk to him. Another gentleman on deputation with this company, who was Company Commandant in the Bihar Military Police, used to also come with him. His name was Kishanji Singh, who later on, in Ánanda Márga, was known as Ácárya Kishanji.

During these evening meetings, the talks were between we three but I was mostly a listener and the topics were spiritual practice and philosophy. Shri Kishanji would base his views on a book called *Satyárth Prakásh* and the thoughts of Ácárya Shivashankar Bandopadhyay were: "The soul is immortal. Life and death happens only to the mind. Bodiless mind can do no work. The idea of ghosts, etc. has no logical basis". Many such topics were discussed and I always found the ideas of Shivashankar very reasonable.

One day I asked him from what sources he got such ideas and asked, "What is the source of all these thoughts? In which book do you find the spiritual communion of this great personality?"

His reply was brief. He said that he had been initiated; he had a guru, and he does sádhaná according to the instructions of his guru. A very keen desire arose in me to go to that guru. But as per the rules at that time, Shivashankarji expressed his inability to take me. He said that as per instructions, without the permission of his guru, neither he could take me to Him, nor give His address. On my request, he went to Jamalpur, and I then got permission to go there and he gave me all the details of the place and the necessary directions to get there.

From Dumka I had to go to Jamalpur, as the person to whom I had been directed to go was at that time staying in Jamalpur. In my mind I accepted Him

as my guru and left for Jamalpur. Shrii Shivashankarji had told me that His name was Shrii Prabhát Raiñjan Sarkar and said that He lived in the Keshavpur locality of Jamalpur. He had given me the details of how to get to Keshavpur from the station, the road in Keshavpur and the description of that particular house and the houses to the left and right, etc. I had never previously been to Jamalpur and so I prepared a mental map of that house and that locality. This was my first visit, and the most important visit, that altogether changed the course of my life.

Jamalpur is a small town in Monghyr District. There is a big Railway Workshop there. This is a junction on the loop line on the track from Howrah, the main station in Calcutta, to the cities of Sahibganj and Bhagalpur in Bihar, from where trains leave for the nearby district headquarters in Monghyr.

Shrii Prabhát Raiñjan Sarkar was working in the Accounts Division of the Railway Workshop. All His comrades and colleagues considered Him an extraordinary personality. People thought that He knew everything, that He could stop coming events, divert coming calamities, could cure diseases and in fact, do everything. But all these things had no importance for me. I wanted a guru. I had been searching a guru who could satisfy my mind, for a long time. I wanted a spiritual guide. Before this whenever I had tried to approach someone to become my guru, different obstacles came up, and either I could not reach there, or was not satisfied after reaching there. No obstacle came in the way of my going to Jamalpur or reaching the designated place there. Moreover, the way I had come into contact with Shivashankarji, the ease with which I got permission, and the way I could find the house, all indicated that everything was going according to a plan, that everything was pre-ordained. The satisfaction I got there with Him is indescribable. How can I describe, when, ‘The eyes have no tongue and the tongue has no eyes’¹⁰?

For about five or six years before I was initiated, I had been in search of a person who could teach me spiritual practice and give me peace of mind. I was financially secure and was happy as a married person, and was only hungry for Self-realization. During this search I came into contact with various monks and saints, but no one could satisfy me nor could I manage to get initiated into any spiritual practice. Sometimes I could not even reach a place designated for initiation, as if some mysterious power was holding me back, until that day in Jamalpur when I was finally initiated.

About two years before my initiation I went to one such holy person, simple and unassuming, whose life as a mendicant had influenced me. We had been friends for a long time. That day I was alone with him in his cottage and he said, “Human life is valuable. It should be properly utilized. Going on pilgrimages counting the rosary and other such things; the application on the forehead of caste-marks and sandal paste (tilaka); the worship of idols and many kinds of long rituals (anuïíhán); these things cannot give Self-realization. Why should one waste his time in these things?”

I asked, “Then is there anything to be done?”

He replied, “Yes, there is a way”. I desired to tread that path and wanted to learn from him the art of Self-realization. Pondering my request he became silent for some time, then he said in Bhojpuri⁶ language, “When the time comes, you will get it”.

This saint known as Mahátmaji in Vaishali was Vajika⁷-speaking. But his guru known as Maháráji⁸ belonged to the Balia area of Uttar Pradesh⁹ and was Bhojpuri¹⁰ speaking. Mahátmaji always spoke in Bhojpuri with everyone. This was symbolic of his devotion, respect and surrender to his guru.

I have already stated that I had never before gone to Jamalpur. Shri Shivashankar Banerjee, from whom I came to know about Bábá, had given me directions how to get from the railway station to Bábá’s house and the details of neighbouring houses. Bábá lived in the Keshavpur locality in a rented house and when I got down at the station I went on foot to the Keshavpur area and stopped before a house. I felt in my mind that this was the house. There was a raised platform near the road, and I climbed the stairs and stood before the gate. A young man was sitting on a chair in the front room, and he asked, “For whom are you looking?”

I said, “I want to see Prabhát Raiôjan Sarkar”. The young man asked me to come in and sit down in the room. I entered the room and sat on another chair that was there whilst this gentleman went in to inform Bábá. This was Bábá’s younger brother Kanhai.

As I sat on the chair, I looked carefully around the room. The house was south facing and the road in front went from east to west. The room where I was sitting had two entrances. One for entering from outside and the other led into the house. Bábá entered through this second door from an inside room.

In the room there were two chairs against the western wall facing east. There was a small table between the two chairs, which was covered by a tablecloth and in front of them against the eastern wall; there was a narrow wooden cot with a durry, or cotton rug, spread over it. Between the chairs and table on one side and the cot on the other side, there was just enough space for walking through the room. On the southern wall over the street entrance there was a photo of Mahatma Gandhi and in the western wall there was a small window. I was sitting near this window.

To the south, in front of the house, there was a lamppost under which all kinds of people used to wait for darshan¹¹. Some of these people looked like beggars or mad people. Ácárya Rameshwar Bethaji had been a class fellow of Bábá’s younger brother Mánasda, and later he took initiation and became an ácárya. His ancestral home was in the same block to the east of Bábá’s house and he knows a lot about the unusual people who used to congregate at the lamp post. On the left of the lamppost on the eastern wall was the board of Dr. J.C.Bose. To the west of Bábá’s house there was a drain about six feet deep. All these later became well-known landmarks for people to find Bábá’s house.

Before Bábá entered this room I heard Him say in Bengali, “Has the time come?” As I heard these words, I remembered the words of the saint in Bhojpuri, “When the time comes you will get it”. Bábá spoke Bengali as He advocates that people use their mother-tongue whenever possible, and then He entered the room and I stood up and did my praòám.

Bábá responded to my praòám, asked me to sit down and then asked, “How have you come. What are your questions? Let me know. I am not a scholar but I shall reply to the best of my capacity”.

I said, “I have not come for asking questions. I have come here in search of guru. I am before you looking at you as my guru. I want to be initiated”.

When I had said this, He changed his tone. He ordered me to close the door and the windows and to sit near Him. Instead of the polite pronoun, ‘aap’, He began using the familiar ‘tum’¹². Following His instructions I sat on the cot in front of Him. The cot was near His chair as described, and the cotton durry already laid on it seemed to indicate that everything was pre-prepared.

Bannerjee-Babu¹³ had told me that I would have to give up non-vegetarian food and take to vegetarianism. I had vowed before going to Bábá that I would only take vegetarian and sentient¹⁴ food, and by His grace right up till today I am strictly vegetarian.

I had no shikha (a tuft of hair on the head kept by high caste Hindus) but even though I had no faith in the sacred thread, (another symbol of high caste)¹⁵, yet due to mental weakness I was still wearing it like a burden. As soon as I was instructed to give it up, I found that I suddenly had the mental strength to remove it and I placed it on the grill of the window by my side as directed.

Bábá told me to give up the distinction between different castes. He taught me that every living creature is the progeny of God. As

my mental weakness was removed I felt a sense of happiness. Then I received initiation. What I was searching for many years, I got there. Completely satisfied and with a happy mind, I left that place.

Before I left, Bábá had said, “You have enjoyed mental pleasure in many previous lives, if you get not do it for one life nothing will be amiss”. Then He became serious and said, “Protect the pink of my face”¹⁶. These words always keep ringing in my mind.

Bábá pointed to the picture of Mahátmá Gandhi above the street door and said, “He was a great person but in the last moments of life he showed some signs of mental weakness. That mental force with which he directed the freedom struggle was not put into application to oppose the division of the country”. These words too, continued ringing in my ears. What I understood from this statement of His, was that my conduct should always be according to Yama and Niyama and pure. That I should never go astray from the ideals and that people will make their conclusions about Him and judge my guru from my conduct.

He also had told me some important things about philosophy and the material world. He asked, “From where did the Swan (soul) come? In what is it absorbed?” Then He answered Himself:

> “The swan has come from the Nirguṇa Brahma¹⁷.
> It is involved in the Saguṇa Brahma¹⁸.
> The swan is wrapped by the body,
> It is lost in máyá (delusion)¹⁹”.

When He gave His permission to leave and I was about to rise from my seat, Bábá said, “You have a small life span but do not bother about it. I shall look after this aspect”.

How He cared for my life and how my life span was lengthened, I shall relate later in the appropriate place. I got up from the cot and did saśt́áuṋga prańáma²⁰ and then as I was leaving Bábá said, “When you come next I shall take you with me for a walk”.

As I described previously, I was posted in Dumka at the Santhal Subdivisional headquarters. After returning home I started practicing sádhaná twice a day with enthusiasm. Closing the doors of the room I was doing sádhaná, as Bábá had instructed to do sádhaná in a separate lonely place but had also added, “For being alone one need not go to a jungle, or mountain. Just by closing the door you will be alone”.

My change of diet pleased my wife as she had already left non-vegetarian food, but when I started closing the door she became curious about the nature of this sádhaná. She was a devout believer in God and also wanted to find the correct method of worshipping God. Finally one day she enquired about what type of sádhaná I was doing and whether women could also practice it. I told her that I would enquire from Gurudev, as I was calling Bábá in those days. When she said that she wanted to go to Gurudev I told her I was unable to take her, as it was His instruction that no one could be taken to Him nor given His address without His prior permission.

By that time I had already been transferred to Bhagalpur, and following the rule, I went to Jamalpur from Bhagalpur and told Bábá everything. He gave His permission for my wife to come and fixed a date for her initiation some ten days later. But on the very next day a Muslim gentleman from Bábá’s own office came as a messenger, bringing a written instruction from Bábá that we must go the very next day for my wife’s initiation. We were already pleased that permission had been given for her initiation, and now our happiness

increased further that it was to be even sooner than initially arranged.

My wife was initiated on the appointed date and in 1955 when the appropriate time came, she was appointed an ácáryá. She became the first lady to receive initiation from Shrii Shrii Ánandamúrtijii when she was initiated in 1954 and later she also became the first lady ácáryá.

When Bábá first told my wife that she would be required to work as an ácáryá, she asked Bábá how would she work as an ácáryá when she was not very educated and did not know anything.

Bábá then replied, “I shall tell you everything. You will know all”.

Bábá told her, “Even if the biggest scholar comes to you, you should consider yourself as her or his ácáryá (teacher)”. After telling her all that was necessary she was appointed the first lady ácáryá in the Márga, Ácáryá Rampari Devi.

One of my relatives who was also a close friend since childhood, was posted as the head of the Central Excise Department in Bhagalpur. The Santhal sub-division was also under him, and whenever he came to Dumka he always stayed with me. When he first came on tour after my initiation and stayed with me, he found a lot of change in the atmosphere there. To see me eating vegetarian food and doing sádhaná in alone, astonished him somewhat. To the best of my ability I explained things to him but the next morning when I was ready to do my sádhaná he smiled and said, “So you are going to turn on the switch of the radio to God!”

One day I went to Bhagalpur on duty and stayed with this relative. He made arrangements for my pújá²¹ with a lot of interest. It was evening and I took some time in sádhaná. Meanwhile my friend was taking rest in his room and was waiting when I finished

![Ac. Chandranath and Aca'rya Rampari Devi](image_placeholder.jpg)

my pújá so that we could eat together. While resting he had a dreamlike experiences in which he saw a shadowy figure. When I finished my sádhaná we sat for dinner and during conversation my friend told me that while he was resting, he had seen a gentlemanly figure saying, “Get up. Time is running away. How long you will be asleep? Do something useful!” and other things in this vein.

When I asked my friend whom had he seen in his vision, whether it was some monk or sádhu²², he said that it was not a monk but an unknown person dressed as a gentleman²³.

Today we can say in terms of Prabhát Saîgiita²⁴, “One un-

known traveller had come”.

I told my friend that the description he had given of that gentleman and how he was dressed etc. described my Gurudev exactly. On that day my friend looked serious and expressed his desire for initiation. I laughed and said, “Then you will also have to tune your radio with God”. But he remained serious and insisted so much that I had to go to Jamalpur and obtain permission for him to go for initiation.

This friend of mine was Nagina Prasad Singh. In the Márga he is known as Ácárya Naginaji. He received permission for initiation after the rains in 1953, but he was initiated on 3^rd November. It took such a long time for Naginaji to go to Jamalpur for many reasons, but over time, a large number of people were inspired by him and took initiation. Amongst them, the following deserve special mention: Ácárya Vishwanath Jha, Ácárya Deep Narayan, Ácárya Shripatiji who later on became Ácárya Shraddhánandji Avadhúta, Ácárya Asthanaji, Bindeshwariji, Lalanji, and others. Ácárya Ganga Sarangiji had initiated him and so was his Shikïa bhai²⁵, but Bábá himself initiated the rest of them.

Naginaji had deep and abiding faith and devotion in Bábá and all these people did commendable work for Ánanda Márga particularly in the earlier stages. The areas that he worked in were Muzaffarpur, Hajipur and Gorakhpur. By nature very kind himself, Bábá was also particularly kind to him. He is famous and popular amongst Márgiis as the author of Ánanda Kathá. In Ánanda Kathá he has described the incident of “The Unknown Traveller”.

All these events occurred prior to 1955. On the 1st of January 1955, all who had been initiated so far, heard Bábá’s discourse in the night and then arranged for a common dinner. This festivity was organized in Jamalpur according to Bábá’s instructions, on the first day of the international calendar. During this first Dharma Mahá Cakra, the topic of the discourse was ‘*The Gradual Development of Society*’

After this, a weekly Dharma Mahá Cakra was held, which later on became monthly, on every full moon day.

As I have said, I had come to Bhagalpur from Dumka by the end of 1953 and there was a story behind this transfer, which was an example of Bábá’s special grace, which I shall write about later.

Now in 1955 on the full moon day of the month of Vaeshákha, (7^th May 1955), Bábá’s birthday was observed for the first time by a Dharma Mahá Cakra in the palace of the Raja Saheb of Monghyr. On that occasion Bábá gave a discourse and again a common meal was arranged.

Although the first Dharma Mahá Cakra was not held until 1^st January 1955, it was actually in November 1954 that we people had begun to come into contact with one another, Bábá gave His first pravachan (discourse) on 11^th November of that year, at which we were all present. After that Bábá began giving a discourse each Sunday then after some time it started taking place once a month. On 1^st January 1955 it began to be called Dharma Mahá Cakra. That is why I have mentioned that the first DMC was held in January 1955.

In this DMC Bábá gave His discourse in a quarter in the Rampur railway colony in Jamalpur that had been allotted to Bábá’s brother Kanhaidá²⁶. All our programmes were held in that quarter. By Bábá’s grace we were using this house as jágrti as He Himself was staying in a rented house with His revered mother and younger

brothers. One of the younger brothers, Himanshuji, was staying in Sahebganj.

After the first DMC, the second DMC was held on 9^th January 1955 also in this house in Rampur Colony and the subject of discourse was “Prakrti Tattva and Oṋkára Tattva”.

The first Dharma Mahá Cakra held outside Jamalpur was organized at my residence in Bhagalpur in February 1955 and after this DMCs started being held every month. This DMC was held on the full moon day of the month of Mágha on 6^th February 1955 and the subject of discourse in the DMC was ‘*Karma & Karmaphala*’ (‘*Action and its Fruit*’). This was extreme grace of Bábá.

My residence in Bhagalpur was in the sergeant major’s quarter in the Police Lines. My wife and I had already been initiated and on that day Bábá initiated our children, my aunt Mangala Devi who was more than eighty years old and our domestic assistant, Makhanji, who was completely illiterate. Only a few people were present at this DMC, for the number of Márgiis was also very small. All the people stayed in this house, Bábá gave His discourse there and after that a common feast was also held there. Up to that time, no ácáryas had been made and still without Bábá’s permission no one could be sent to Him, and permission was not granted to everyone.

After DMC Bábá returned to Jamalpur the same day. Before He left, I requested Bábá to stay over and leave the next day.

Bábá lovingly said, “You see, I have stayed more than twelve hours. I have never before stayed in anyone’s house that long. Now that you have requested me to stay, we shall see on some other occasion”. As I will relate later, Bábá fulfilled my desire.

Three DMCs were held in this house in all and I shall give more details of the specialties of this house and about food and other things later. Bábá’s next visit in that house was in July 1955. On the occasion of this DMC, the number of Márgiis had increased but even then everyone could be accommodated there. DMC and food were also arranged there itself. By then we had been made ácáryas by Bábá. The first ácárya was Pranay Kumar Chatterjee. He was also working as General Secretary of Ánanda Márga. Bábá had very kindly appointed six of us as ácáryas and given us the responsibility of initiating people. Instructions at that time were that only those should be initiated who have keen desire.

In July 1955, at the time of this DMC many people came to take initiation and the ácáryas gave them initiation. A large number of aspirants had come for initiation in order to see Bábá. They were being attracted like rivers mingling in the ocean. On this occasion as Bábá reached our house and sat on the appointed places eighty year old my aunt, crawled on the ground and reaching Him, she placed her head on His feet. That aunt of mine was full of devotion and respect was very strict in sádhaná and would never take her food without doing her sádhaná. She would always either take a bath or half-bath before sádhaná. After blessing her after her praṇám Bábá told her to do maximum sádhaná. My aunt said, “’What can I do now? I am very tired”. Bábá said, “Do as much as you can and the rest I shall do”.

Now see Bábá would always say that following Dharma should begin from childhood itself. It should not be postponed for old age. Guardians should always attend to this matter.

My aunt became increasingly weak and in about four months left this world. Some days before my aunt’s death, my wife went to Jamalpur for Bábá’s darshan and Bábá asked after my aunt.

Ácáryáji (my wife had become ácáryá by then) said, “Aunty has become very weak, she does not feel hungry and eats very little”. Bábá then said, “In the last days of life this happens. All right, send her to me”.

On our return Ácáryáji told me that Bábá has called auntiji and told aunty about this. Aunty asked, “In this condition how shall I go to Bábá?”

I said, “I shall arrange a car”. I started planning to take her to Bábá by car and was applying for leave, but aunty left the world within two or three days. Then I understood what Bábá meant by sending her to Him!

Bábá had told aunty, “Whatever remains I shall do for you”. When aunty had felt that life was about to ebb out, she called me from my office and when I reached her, she kissed and fondled me, put her hand on my head and blessed me. As she wanted to lie down, I helped her to her bed with the help of my domestic assistant, Makhanji. Within half an hour of this the whole story was over. She left in so much peace. Neither any illness, nor any pain; simply felt like going and was gone. On her deathbed, her breathing became weak and slow. She closed her eyes after looking at the photo of Bábá in barábhaya mudrá. She was unable to speak but still there was life within and her hearing was intact. When I asked whether she remembered the mantra, she moved her head affirmatively. In a few moments she began to breathe heavily and then her breathing stopped and her head rolled over on one side. The bird of mind had flown away. The whole ordeal was finished in half an hour.

It was only two or three days before that Bábá had told my wife to send aunty to Him and had told her “I shall do the rest”. Bábá completed the rest and she went to Bábá.

No one should make fun that I am describing miracles. I do not attach any importance to miracles. While writing my reminiscence whatever comes to my mind, I have written that. Miracles are small things. Táraka Brahma establishes ideals while remaining in human form and he established his ideology in that way. In this process humans are changed into Gods and thus Dharma is established in society. All that I have written here indicates this. I have already stated how much one can know and how much one can write. I have written only a part of what I saw, heard and understood and when the opportunity comes I will state more.

To understand and write everything is not only difficult, it is also impossible for one man. “God is infinite and innumerable are tales about Him”. This is absolutely true. He can only be known by His own grace.

> “Only they know, whom You enable to do so;
> Once one knows You – they become You”.

# CHAPTER FIVE

After 1953 March I started sádhaná regularly both times a day – morning and evening sincerely and as told by Bábá I also continued to get spiritual experiences. But as instructed by Bábá I never gave any importance to them.

Bábá had said, “Some experiences will be frightening and some will give you a feeling of pride, but you remain fearless and humble. These spiritual experiences have no meaning in a sádhaka's life. They are milestones. Never pay attention to them. Go on walking on your own path. Never describe your experiences before anyone”.

I continued sádhaná and my self-confidence continued to grow. I was ever peaceful and happy and my work efficiency also increased. This increased my respect and estimate in the eyes of my colleagues and officers. People would be curious to know and came to me and during off time spiritual topics always came up for conversation. It was these talks that gave an ácárya like Naginaji to the Márga, which I have already described. Not only Naginaji, but these conversations on spiritual topics also gave ácáryas like Kishanji to the Márga who worked night and day for dharma pracar. Time thus passed blissfully and this life of sádhaná and satsauôga continued for about six months.

In the month of October suddenly my transfer orders were received. I had to go to Purnea. My mind was disturbed. Dumka and Jamalpur took about six hours, but from Purnea to Jamalpur it took almost the whole day. For going to Jamalpur from Purnea one had to cross the Ganges on a steamer those days. I felt the infinite grace of the Guru. My disturbed mind was filled with joy and this is

how things transpired.

In Dumka district a new Superintendent had been posted. He had just taken over the charge. He did not want to relieve me. He requested the Inspector General to retain me for sometime on the same post. (In those days a separate post of Inspector General had not been created and the same Inspector General was all in all in the Police Department). I received the stay order through wireless. But events took such a turn in the Police Department of Bhagalpur that the Superintendent of Police and the Sergeant Major had to go there soon. The new Police Superintendent took charge. I also received my transfer orders very soon. I assumed charge in the same month with great pleasure. Jamalpur was only one and a half hour’s journey from here.

All the departmental colleagues considered the new Superintendent of Police of Bhagalpur strict and dry, but I found him very straightforward and sincere in his work. He expected the same qualities in his subordinates and the two of us pulled on very well. He gave me due respect and I also developed respect for him in my heart.

As per rules permission had to be obtained for leaving headquarters and so every Saturday I used to take written permission from him on going to Jamalpur and have guru darshan. I used to go on Sunday morning and came back in the night. Wherever he was; in office or at home, the messenger would soon return with his approval of my leave application. Everyone was surprised. They used to say that Kumar Sahib, as they used to call me, has hypnotized Chatterjee Sahib, the new Police Superintendent. He has been charmed. What could I say? Bábá krpáhi kevalam!

One day the Police Superintendent told me, “Why do you send application to leave headquarters every Sunday?”

I said, “I go to Jamalpur. That is where my Guru lives. I go to see Him and hear His discourse”.

Then he said, “You are going for a sacred work. It’s all right. Now there is no need for application. Just for my information you will give a telephone call that you are going to Jamalpur”.

Like this in good pleasure and peace 1953 was over.

In 1954 in the first four months I went to my village home on leave. From there once I came to Jamalpur once in between for guru darshan. In the night after the walk I sat on the tiger’s grave like before. On this grave one had to sit with Bábá. When for the first time after initiation I went to the grave with Bábá, I wanted to sit down - thinking how can I sit on the same seat with Bábá. But Bábá instructed me sit by his side on the cemented platform. He said that this is the rule for sitting on the grave.

I followed the instruction of the guru.

Bábá very lovingly asked “Do you hear some sweet musical sound at the time of sádhaná?”

I replied in the affirmative. Then He explained some philosophy to me. Ánanda Márga was still not then established nor had any literature of the Márga been published. Bábá explained the philosophy clearly. He said that in the cosmic mind in its saiñcara process, first of all the great open space (sky) was created. It has only one tanmátra or physical emanation or inference, the “sound” inference. Therefore in the created Universe, this is the subtlest creation.

After the establishment of Ánanda Márga in 1955, the primary philosophy was published in the name of “Ánanda Márga” so that this philosophy became available to all.

Bábá told me that this sound pervading the whole open space is called “Oîkar”. During sádhaná, when the propensities of the mind are directed inward they hear “Oîkar sound”, Bábá described different levels of hearing the sound and explained that as one nears the Paramá Puruṋa during sádhaná, this sound becomes clearer.

In the end this sound will come as if in the zero hours of the night a flute sound is coming attractively and sweet and one most pleasant to the mind. This will attract the mind, one will get bliss during sádhaná, the concentration of mind will grow increasingly and progressively and lastly one will attain proximity with the Parama Puruṋa. So saying, Bábá touched the portion behind the svádhiśt́hána cakra and below the spiritual cord. With this touch the whole of my body was vibrated. This vibration gave me immense bliss and I began to hear the sweet sound of flute coming from a distance with my ears.

Bábá said, “Now during sádhaná, you will hear this sound and after some time whenever you want you will hear it. Devotees call this the flute of Kŕśńa.”

The second day I returned to my village where I was doing sádhaná and feeling bliss. But from here, struggle for the ideal also began in my life. That night Bábá had said “I shall test you from time to time. Be cautious”. I shall describe details later.

As soon as my leave finished, I returned to Bhagalpur and resumed my work. My daily routine started as usual. Doing sádhaná, following Yama and Niyama and discussing the spiritual matters heard from Bábá and going to Jamalpur for guru darshan, these were my activities those days.

Once while I had gone to Jamalpur from Bhagalpur. I went for a walk with Bábá in the evening and sat on the grave of the tiger. Bábá said, “Look here, there is a poem from Rabindranath Tagore:

> “Hey! Bird! Oh my bird, do not stop moving your wings yet!”

Then Bábá said, “A bird while flying on the ocean, went a little too far. He began to feel tired. The bird returned towards the shore. It was feeling very tired, evening was advancing; the darkness was about to descend. The shore is still far off. If due to tiresomeness the bird stops flying then it will fall into the sea and drown. Therefore it had to continue flying, advancing towards the shore as the darkness was about to descend. His inner soul told him, “Do not stop flying, go on advancing further and further”.

The purpose of this story and its moral is that people may face a large number of obstacles, but should still move on towards their goal. By losing courage, one does not have to drown in the ocean.

Later on I looked for the whole of that poem of Rabindranath Tagore and I am putting down the meanings here for the readers.

### *DUKH SAMAY (TIMES OF SORROW)*

Although gradually the evening of life is approaching, on someone’s indication, the music of life has stopped. In this infinite sky, no companion or friend is left with me, the body is feeling tired. Inside me, the feeling of a great impending danger is arising. All

corners of the sky are covered in the shroud of darkness. Even so, Oh my life bird! Do not stop your wings from flying – do not fold them.

The road of life is not always full of the musical murmur of forest sounds. Here the sound of the python, its angry hissing comes from the waves. Here there is no bower full of multi-petalled jasmine. Here there is the roar of foamy giant waves and their strong vibrations. Where is that shore beautified by flowers and where is that nest? That supporting branch and twigs – where are they? Even so, Oh bird! Do not fold up your wings – do not stop flying.

Even now the long night is before you. The bright morn is sleeping in the distant evening sky. The whole world is waiting with bated breath and sitting unmoved in loneliness – waiting for the light of the morning and counting moments and hours. The south moon has risen by tearing the darkness of the night, Oh my dearest bird! Do not fold up your wings yet!

Up upon the sky the stars are making gestures at you with their fingers. Down in the deep, volatile death is attacking you with a hundred rising waves. In the distant shore, someone is calling you in pathetic notes, with folded hands: “Come! Come! Oh my dear bird! Do not fold your wings yet!”

Now there is no fear! No bonds of love and passion. There is no hope. Hope is really a deception. There is no language – no words. No futile crying for someone. There is no home! No flowerbeds. Now there are only wings and there is this immense courtyard of the great sky to fly. The dawn has lost its way! All directions are drowned in deep darkness.

Oh my bird! Oh my life bird! Oh my dear, dear bird, do not despair in this hour of crisis. Do not rest in this dark hour. Do not close your eyes and become blind. Do not fold your wings. You have to move on and on!

When there is no person and place, there is no time either.

People derive pleasure or want to derive pleasure from small and large things. But one cannot get unending pleasure from relative truths. Therefore the wise do sádhaná only to realize the Timeless Entity. Body and mind are not beyond time. To run after them is foolishness. They must be properly looked after, but they are not to be the object of sádhaná. One does sádhaná to be established in the Timeless Entity.

During these early days there was great speed in all projects and every way. We could neither think nor understand how there was so much speed in fulfilling all the objectives. On the appointed date we reached Gaddopur where the Tattva Sabha had been organised in a mango grove in the west of the village. This mango grove is in front of a temple, which is on the place of a samádhi² of a sannyásii - named Keshavanandaji. This temple stands on the ground where Keshavanandaji was cremated and was constructed in the twelfth century since when the village people had been attending to its maintenance.

The Tattva Sabha was attended by almost all the people of the village, young and old. The village people sat in front on one side and separately the group of pandits sat in front of us. They had turbans on their heads, long cloths around their necks, wearing dhotis and kurta and sitting up straight and seriously, all in a sombre mood. There are Brahmins in my village whose main profession is doing the work of purohits (priests). There were many Saḿskrta scholars amongst them. Some of these purohits could speak and lecture in Saḿskrta most proficiently and they were given the title, Suvaktaji, (one who speaks well), by the people; the pandits had gathered under their leadership.

Both the lady ácáryás went out in the village from door to door to meet the village ladies in their houses. This was what Bábá had instructed the lady ácáryás to do, and I had conveyed this to them. The three ácáryas delivered their lectures, and by Bábá’s grace the meeting was perfectly disciplined and everyone was quiet and attentive.

When the lectures were over I invited questions from the audience. Suvaktaji came forward and asked: -

(1) “Why do you people oppose idol worship?”
(2) “Why don’t you accept caste differences?”
(3) “Why do you approve of giving up of the sacred thread and the shikha (tuft of hair at the top of the head maintained by high caste Hindus)?”

After remembering and meditating on the guru, I replied to all the three questions one by one with full self-confidence. Whatever I said in detail can be summed up briefly as below: -

(1) “We do not want to hurt anyone’s feelings by opposing idol worship. Yes, we do not support it as it is not essential for Brahma sádhaná, (attaining the ultimate reality). We actually want to give priority to what the Vedas say and what other scriptures maintain.

Brahma (the ultimate reality) is a beginningless, infinite and all pervading entity. Such an entity cannot be restricted within any limits. Now how can there be an idol representing that which is limitless? No, it cannot be limited to an idol. The Vedas also prove this. Earlier, the speaker Hari Sadhanji recited from the Vedas the following: -

‘The Final Truth takes and holds everything, though having no physical hands or feet. He sees everything without eyes and hears without ears. He is the knower and the object of all knowledge. There is none who knows Him. Only this Truth is the greatest subject of all’.

According to this, the Ultimate Truth has no physical body. I again explained that there could be no idol of Parama Puruśa. Therefore idol worship is not the exercise to attain Brahma. It is the imagination of people who constructed various idols of gods and goddesses described in the Puráńas (mythologies)³. In this process, due to ignorance, they forgot the worship of God and began to worship the idol. We reminded everyone of this fact. For attaining Brahma, Ánanda Márga preaches the practice of Aśtáuṋga Yoga (eight-fold path of yoga). Mahárshi Patañjali has described this in his book *Yoga Shástra*. We treat the Vedas and traditional scriptures as evidential fact and proof of experience, and nowhere in the Vedas or scriptures has idol worship been recommended.

The author of the Puráńas, Vyasaji himself, confessed his mistake and said: -

> “*I restricted you, O Infinite, in shapes, bodies;*
> *I confined you to various tirthas (places of pilgrimage),*
> *You are beyond all description and speech yet I have restricted you in words.*
> *Lord of the Universe! Please pardon these three faults of mine!*
>
> *You who are beyond and without shape, I have imagined you in shape;*
> *You are indescribable, yet I have ignored Your indescribable nature and prayed to You in speech;*
> *You are everywhere yet I have ignored that fact and described you as residing at the holy places.*
> *O Lord! Kindly forgive my weakness and these three faults of mine*’”.

Therefore it is proved that by making an idol and worshipping it as God, amounts to negating His existence. Therefore idol worship is a kind of mistake.

(2) We do not support castism, as all are the children of one universal God. Everyone’s father is the Supreme Lord - Paramá Pita. Therefore all are equal. The Vedas state that the whole cre-

ation has emanated from God - it exists in Him and it finally merges in Him. The creator has created the world and He is hidden in that. There is one Rudra⁴ or ultimate destroyer. There is no second. He is the Lord of all. He creates and pervades the creation and in the end in Him all vanishes. He remains hidden in His own creatures.

If those who follow the Vedas do not believe in caste distinctions, will it therefore be time to say that they do not respect the Vedas? The traditional scriptures do not subscribe to distinctions of castes. The Brahma Sutra⁵ written by Veda Vyasa also states that the whole world has emanated from God. The Yoga Shástra by Patañjali shows the way to attain Brahma. And the methods described are not for any particular caste nor is there any distinction made between men or women. All are equally eligible for sádhaná.

Thus we see that in the Vedas and scriptures, caste distinctions are not mentioned in any way. We respect the Vedas and the scriptures. The Vedas contain spiritual science as well as worldly knowledge.

Worldly knowledge is related to time, space and person, to the entities that are subject to change. Therefore worldly knowledge is necessarily changeable. The way food is prepared and eaten, the way to make and wear clothes, the way houses are constructed, all these arts have changed from ancient times until today according to changing needs. The science of war, the kind of vehicles for transport, all these have been changing as the needs of the times have changed. But Brahma is one beginningless, infinite and all-pervading entity. He is unchangeable. He is beyond time, space and person. Really all these are dependent on Brahma. They are not absolute truths. They are only relative truths. Brahma alone is Absolute Truth. Ádi Shaunkarácarya⁶ also has said as much.

Thus we see that knowledge relating to Brahma is perennial and unchangeable. It is today as it was in ancient times, and it will remain as such in future. We fully adhere to the spiritual knowledge as stated in the Vedas. Therefore not accepting caste system is in fact according to the Veda and the traditional scriptures.

Let us think again what direction all our great sages have given! Bhagaván Shaunkara is the first guru of Yoga and Tantra on this earth. He systematised all available knowledge of Tantra and Yoga and made it accessible to the ordinary people. He made it acceptable to one and all. He never made distinctions between castes or gender in doing this. According to him, all that is necessary for sádhaná is to have a human body. So once one has attained a human body one has attained everything one requires. So we are really fortunate to have received the human body. Come! Let us make good use of it. Let us not waste human birth.

From materials created by Itself, the Infinite creates a body from time to time to protect the good and virtuous people, and comes on earth in human form. Such an advent is called Mahásambhúti (Great Incarnation). Even today, Sadáshiva, (the first guru of Tantra), is worshipped by all, everywhere in the country. Let us also think of the teachings of Bhagaván Shri Krsna who came on this earth as the second great advent. He said that he comes from age to age to protect the sádhus (good people) and to establish righteousness.

> “*To protect the virtuous and for destroying the wicked and to establish Dharma, I come from age to age*”⁷.

He does not come for a particular caste. For Him, caste has no meaning. All are equal for Him. Who is a sádhu? By being born in a particular caste or class, one does not become a sádhu. A sádhu is determined by action. It is written in the shástras: -

‘*As one loves his own life*
*So other creatures do similarly*
*A sádhu is one who loves and cares*
*For all creatures, as he does for his own*’

As we love our life, so do other creatures love their own. Therefore considering all creatures as their own, sádhus are kind towards all. God comes for such sádhus. Where is the place of castes in this? The correct path is that which the sages have demonstrated. Dharmaráj Yudhisthira⁸ said: -

‘*That is the path which the Great Ones travel*’.

In the present age there is another Great Incarnation. He is called Ánandamúrtijii and through the medium of this name, He is giving the light of Brahmavidyá⁹ (the path leading to the realization of Brahma), in this world. He is teaching all the sádhaná processes of Aiîauôga Yoga (eight-fold path of yoga). Here there is no distinction on the basis of caste or sex. I have with me three ácáryas and two lady ácáryás. All have come as representatives of Ánandamúrtijii to each aspirant and to all who desire to learn the eight-fold path of yoga. Everyone can learn it without any distinction. They may belong to any caste, may be young or old, male or female, all are eligible. Those who desire may come and learn.

(3) The shikha and the sacred thread are discriminatory. In this age they are unnecessary. When we do not believe in the caste system, the distinctive marks of high and low have no purpose and need not be worn. In olden times, yajiôa or sacrifices were performed. In these, the gods were beseeched through Vedic hymns. An effort was made to appease them though there were no idols of gods as there was no idol worship in those days. Even today those who perform Yoga, do not practice idol worship. In the ancient times, different expressions of God were treated as different gods.

(4) People used to worship God via His different expressions. God was to be propitiated through different mediums. During a yajiôa, good people not only used to meet together, but would also take advantage of thinking and discussing various issues. The responsibility of proper conduct of the yajiôa used to be given to five Brahmins or pious men.

These five Brahmins were called *Hota* (those who offered the sacrifice) *Rtvik*, *Udháryu*, *Udgata* and *Brahmin*. The *Hota* was the controller of the yajiôa. *Rtviks* were those learned in Rgveda. *Udháryu* were well versed in *Yajur Veda*. *Udgata* were singers of the Samaveda and *Brahmins* were helping all of them.

In order to recognise these five types of Brahmins, a distinctive symbol, the deerskin, would hang across their shoulders. This was called the *yajiôa paveet*. I cannot say about other places, but even now in this district of Mithila, before giving the sacred thread, a small piece of deerskin is touched. I asked the pandits and village people whether this was true or not? Suvaktaji remained silent as the leader of the pandit group, but the villagers replied in the affirmative.

Then I also enquired whether the shikha tuft on the top of the head and the sacred thread were not only unnecessary but meaningless? In this context look at the part of the *Nasdiya Sukta* of *Rgveda* known as the *Brahmana Nárada*. It proves the meaninglessness of the sacred thread and the shikha tuft and states: -

1. After shaving the head clean, the learned should also give up the sacred thread. The eternal Brahma is in the form of this

sacred thread – understanding this that Brahma should be established in the heart.
2. The learned person who practices the highest yoga should relinquish the outer sacred thread. The one who wears this sacred thread is the consciousness itself. Merging in Brahma alone is the real wearing of the thread. One who puts on this (awareness of Brahma) never becomes non-pious or dirty.
3. Those who wear the sacred thread of knowledge and who believe in the power of knowledge are purified by knowledge itself.
4. That man who wears knowledge as a sacred thread, for him there is no other sacred thread and they alone are real sacred thread wearers and are known as knowledgeable. Apart from this, those who keep a tuft of hair outside on the top cannot be considered as Shikhudhani.
5. Whose tuft is knowledge alone and sacred thread is also knowledge alone – they alone are fully Brahmins.

After giving replies to all the questions of Suvaktaji, it appeared that he really has no further questions. Therefore he said that yoga sádhaná is not for those who are in the family, it is for those who renounce home. He projected this as a new question. Why do you teach it to those who have a family?

Most humbly I explained that Lord Shiva is the Ádi Guru (first guru) of Yoga and Tantra and was himself a family man, and lived with his family and relations. He taught Yoga sádhaná to one and all without any distinction. According to him, the foremost necessity for learning sádhaná is a human body.

Saint Tulsidas has also said “It is great good fortune to receive a human body. This is rarely available even to the gods”. All great works of holy men say the same thing. And this human body is the abode of sádhaná or the door of salvation. For sádhaná there is no distinction between family people and monks. The great Mahásambhúti Lord Krśńa is also called the Lord of Yogis, and He was also a family man. Mahárshi Patañjali wrote the *Yoga Shástra* and in this book there is no bar against family people learning this science. This is useful for all without any distinction. The great sage Sukhdeva had consulted King Janaka who was a family man and the sage Vashista muni was himself also a family man. All this proves that family people are not only eligible for Yoga, but it is their duty to learn and practice Yoga.

Looking at the loss of Dharma, Parama Puruśa has appeared in the form of a Divine Advent to teach one and all without distinction after breaking all boundaries, in the name and shape of Ánandamúrtijii. He has made Brahma Sádhaná accessible to all human beings.

All these present in the Tattva Sabha approved and agreed that family people are eligible.

Suvaktaji became somewhat angry and aggressive. He shook with emotion. “You people are atheists.”

I asked, “How could that be?”

He replied, “Atheists are those who criticise Vedas. You people practice against the teachings of Vedas.”

I said, “We are doing Astaun̂ga Yoga and sádhaná on the basis of the teachings of the Vedas. According to you, only those who follow idol worship in the name of the worship of Parama Puruśa are believers. We respect the Vedas and the scriptures. We worship Parama Puruśa in his own form and description as per the Vedas. I have explained all this in detail in my speech.”

The pandit flew into a greater rage and going beyond civil dialogue,

he began to abuse us and insult us in very rude language. Immediately an elderly and respectable person, Janaka Kumar, who was a retired teacher, asked the pandit to be quiet.

He said “Panditji we have been hearing you people since our childhood, but we have been like the bullock who goes round and round an oil crushing machine. We like what these young men are saying and they also are logical. We want to hear them don’t make a disturbance please!”

A summary of the substance of what Bábá taught me about spirituality before going to the Tattva Sabha, is as follows:

In the movement of pratisaiṋcara, the thinking powers of the cosmic citta, or mind-stuff, are gradually awakened and increased. As a result, the cosmic mind came into existence. This state of Brahma is called Saguńa Brahma. Brahma is singular and not dual. Nirguńa (unqualified) and saguńa (qualified) are only two states of the same Brahma. Where prakrti or nature is active, cosmic mind becomes expressed and begins functioning. In that state Brahma is called saguńa, or qualified. Where prakrti is sleeping in the subject puruśa (consciousness), the cosmic mind has not come into existence; where there is absolute inaction, that state is called nirguńa (unqualified).

A man in the waking or sleeping state is one and the same person and does not become two. Take one more example: there is a great ocean and in some part of that ocean, gusts of wind create waves, but elsewhere the ocean is quiet. Now the portion under waves and the other, which is peaceful, are not two oceans. It is one and the same. Waves are created by the effect of the wind. The waves and the calm water are the same entity; they exist in the ocean and they merge or disappear in the same ocean.

In the same manner, saguńa and nirguńa are the two states of the same consciousness. Brahma is the combined name of prakrti (nature) and puruśa (Consciousness). Prakrti is the power of the consciousness or puruśa. Both are inseparable just as fire and its burning power are indivisible. Puruśa is the infinite cognitive faculty and prakrti is the infinite actional faculty. Infinite cognitive faculty and infinite power is one - it cannot be two. A second power can only come into existence when the first one is finished or disappears.⁴ Therefore, Brahma is one, beginningless, endless, and all-pervading and both the states of Brahma, the saguńa and nirguńa are infinite.

It is said in the Upaniśads: -

> *Oôḿ pūrńamadah pūrńadam’*
> *Pūrńat pūrńam udcyate*
> *Pūrńasya pūrńámadaya*
> *Pūrńam eva vasisyate*

> *This is infinite and that is infinite*
> *And the total is Infinite.*
> *When we take out of the infinite,*
> *Whatever is taken is infinite,*
> *And what remains is also infinite.*

Modern mathematicians also say that infinity minus infinity = infinity.

Cosmic mind is infinite and therefore its creativity is also infinite. And so this world, though philosophically considered limited, is still theoretically unlimited. Cosmic mind is beginning-less and endless, and therefore its creation is also endless. Creation has been going on since eternity and it will go on until eternity. There may be destruction here and there, but the whole creation will never be finally destroyed.

Prakrti has three attributes, and due to their influence, cosmic

mind also has three parts. With the influence of the sentient principle, awareness of itself came into being. It became aware that ‘*I am*’. This is called mahattattva. Due to the influence of the mutative principle, came the feeling of doership. The awareness came that ‘*I am the doer*’. This is called ahaḿtattva.

As it thinks about a subject or object, the formation of the citta takes place. After this, from the citta of Brahma or Supreme Consciousness, the world came into being.

Thus we see that Cosmic Consciousness converts itself into cosmic mind and from the cosmic mind its citta portion created the world. This is mind’s progress from subtle to crude. This is called the flow of saiñcara. In this flow, the five tattvas or fundamental elements are born. In the thought of the cosmic mind, the citta of Brahma first was metamorphosed into the ethereal principle. Thus space or the sky was created. This was the first crude manifestation of the citta of Brahma. Thus began the creation of the world.

Along with the creation of the five fundamental principles came inferences. In the ether is the inference of sound. The thought of the cosmic mind proceeded further and in the second stage of the continuing flow of saiñcara came the aerial factor. In this was created the inference of touch as well as sound. When the wind blows, we feel its touch. As the flow of saiñcara proceeded further, the luminous factor was created. This carries the inference of form as well as that of sound and touch. The sun contains the fire principle. It has a shape, a form and there is a physical boundary. At the fourth stage in the path of subtle to crude came the liquid factor. Apart from sound, touch and form, this carries the inference of taste. We feel this with our tongue. The crudest manifestation of Brahma citta is the solid factor, which carries, along with the earlier four inferences, the inference of smell. The Earth has all the five inferences i.e. sound, touch, form, taste and smell. The crudity of an object is proportionate to the number of inferences in it. The solid factor is the crudest manifestation of Cosmic Consciousness. This is the end of the process of saiñcara.

The thought process of the cosmic mind goes on uninterrupted. Its progress never stops. This mobility is creation. Creation is manifest only in the thought process of the cosmic mind. The created world exists in that and it finally merges in the same mind.

From here the cosmic mind cannot progress further towards crudity and therefore it takes a turn and starts advancing towards the subtle. This flow from crude to subtle is called prati-saiñcara. In the first stage of this flow towards the subtle, práńáh, or vital energy, arises. In the water, algae begin to accumulate, and on trees and the soil a layer of moss, and grass on the Earth and thereafter the more developed forms of trees bearing flowers and fruits in which we find the signs of life force. But due to the dominance of crudity they do not possess mobility, the power to move from one place to another.

On this path, the thought process reaches further subtlety and we find small forms of life in insects and germs, which have the power to move from one place to another. Further we find more developed forms of life in animals and birds that develop some mind, the power to think. They move in search of food, and search for or make a safe place to live. They also rear their children and protect them and help them to learn how to live.

Finally on the last point of this growth in subtlety, comes the

most developed creature, human beings. Human beings originate in the last part of the flow of Pratisaiñcara. Human beings are creatures with intelligence, they are mind-predominant. In this world human beings alone have the highest form of mind. Because of this, they have the power to think independently. They think freely. Action first takes the form of a thought in human beings, and so they think and act with freedom. Up to the stage of human beings, the development of life is totally dependent on nature. After attaining a human body they think and act independently and therefore are subject to action and reaction.

The cosmic mind is working constantly. Hence there is mobility and growth everywhere in this world. The movement never stops even for a moment; therefore, if human beings do not move forward on this path, they will have to move backward. They will get the next body as a result of the fruits of their actions. If in the pratisaiñcara stream, moving towards subtlety, one does not think freely and does not move towards the cosmic consciousness by ones actions, then one will move backwards in this pratisaiñcara stream and from human stage will go down to animal and bird life, the life of germs and insects and in the form of trees and plants and like this backwards to any extent.

As one can progress by one's progressive actions towards greater subtlety so by dint of faulty actions one can decline to cruder forms of existence. Therefore, it is our duty that we should move towards the cosmic consciousness and try to attain that stage. It is the grace of God, which gives us the human body, and therefore we have an opportunity to move towards Him.

There is a story about this grave built in the big open ground in Jamalpur. In those days it was the British Rule in India. In the Railway Workshop, higher positions were occupied by a large number of Englishmen. There were dense forests around the hills in the town and a large number of wild animals used to live there. One fine day an Englishman was walking in that field and as night fell a tiger came into the field in the dark. There was a fight between the tiger and the Englishman. The tiger attacked him and the Englishman fought to the best of his ability but was killed there. The tiger also sustained serious wounds and so after walking a little way, it fell and died.

The local people made the graves for both the Englishman and the tiger at these two places. The grave in the east is that of the tiger and to the west is that of the Englishman. There is a cemented platform on the tiger’s grave. Bábá would always sit on this platform on the tiger’s grave. It was the accepted custom that every disciple who went there with Bábá would also sit on the grave with Him. I have already stated that when in the beginning I wanted to sit on the ground with the idea that a disciple should not sit on the same level as the Guru, Bábá instructed me to sit with Him on the platform itself. This was the rule He made in that place.
